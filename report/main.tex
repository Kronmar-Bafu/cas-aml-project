\begin{abstract}
Traditional species distribution models treat occurrences as
independent points, ignoring the spatial structure of landscapes.
We adapt the SOM-GNN-SDM framework \citep{Wu2025} to Swiss vascular
plants at 30\,m resolution, constructing a landscape graph of
166,464 patches from 12 environmental features via Self-Organising
Map clustering. A GraphSAGE architecture tuned for sessile flora
models habitat suitability for 2,696 species, outperforming a
Random Forest baseline for 85.3\,\% of species (mean AUC 0.912
vs.\ 0.874). Circuit-theory connectivity analysis across 241
nationally threatened species reveals that 41\,\% of high-priority
habitat---patches combining high suitability with critical
corridor function---currently lacks formal protection. The pipeline
identifies spatially explicit conservation gaps.
\end{abstract}
